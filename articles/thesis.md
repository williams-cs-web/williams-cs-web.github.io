The Computer Science Department offers majors the opportunity to pursue a yearlong independent research project under the guidance of a faculty member. An honors thesis is a chance to explore an area of computer science in depth, engage with current research, and take substantial ownership of a project.

The thesis itself is the most important component of honors work. It should be a significant piece of written work that places the project in the context of previous research, explains the student’s original contributions and results, and discusses remaining questions or future work. Depending on the area, the project may also involve substantial software development.

**Getting started**: Students considering honors should begin talking with faculty members during their junior year, ideally well before spring registration. These conversations are an opportunity to learn about possible projects and find a faculty member whose interests are a good match. Students interested in pursuing honors must also submit a research application by the announced deadline.

Once a student and faculty advisor agree on a project, the student registers for **CSCI 493–W031–494** for senior year. Note: Courses with **9 as the middle digit** (including thesis courses) cannot be counted as a major requirement.

**What to expect**: Honors work begins in the fall semester of senior year. Students initially focus on defining the research problem, reading relevant literature, and beginning the research itself. By the end of the semester, students should have a clear research direction and a substantial written account of the problem and its background.

**Winter Study is a crucial part of the honors year:** With few competing academic demands, January should be an especially productive period. Students should aim to complete most of their original research and software development during Winter Study. During this period, the student and advisor also select a second reader.

Early in the spring semester, the department decides whether the student will formally continue as a candidate for Honors, based on the quality and progress of the work. Students also give a public presentation of their work at a department colloquium.

The remainder of the spring is devoted largely to writing and revision. Students submit chapters to both readers for feedback, followed by a complete thesis draft and a revised final version.

The year concludes with a **thesis defense**: a roughly 30-minute public presentation followed by questions from faculty and other audience members. After the defense, the department considers the student’s work over the course of the year and determines whether it merits Honors, Highest Honors, or no distinction.

**Should I pursue honors?** An honors thesis is an opportunity to work closely with a faculty member, investigate a problem in depth, and experience the kind of sustained research involved in graduate study.

It is also a substantial commitment. The thesis occupies two courses, and Winter Study becomes an intensive period of research rather than a comparatively unstructured month. Students considering honors are encouraged to talk with faculty members and current honors students about the experience before deciding.
