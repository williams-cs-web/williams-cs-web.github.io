The Computer Science Department is delighted to welcome two new faculty members this fall: **Lucas Rosenblatt**, joining us as an Assistant Professor, and **Nate Chambers**, joining us as an Associate Professor.

**Lucas Rosenblatt** comes to Williams from New York University, where he recently completed his Ph.D. in Computer Science. His research focuses on trustworthy and privacy-preserving computing, including differential privacy, algorithmic fairness, and privacy in large language models. At NYU, Lucas was a member of both the Center for Responsible AI and the Theoretical Computer Science Group, and his work has been supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. His research brings together theoretical computer science, machine learning, and questions about how computational systems affect people and society.

Lucas also brings a strong interest in teaching and public engagement with computing. At NYU, he taught Responsible Data Science and helped develop *We Are AI*, a public education project designed to make ideas in artificial intelligence and responsible AI accessible to broader audiences. Before graduate school, he studied computer science and nonfiction writing at Brown University.

**Nate Chambers** joins Williams after fifteen years on the faculty at the United States Naval Academy, where he was a Professor of Computer Science and co-director of the Center for High Performance Computing. Nate is a researcher in natural language processing and artificial intelligence, with a particular interest in how computational systems can learn about events, people, and the world by reading text. His recent work explores the ability of large language models to understand, reason about, and draw inferences from events.

Nate received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from Stanford University and has taught courses across the computer science curriculum, from introductory programming to artificial intelligence and natural language processing. At the Naval Academy, he worked extensively with undergraduate researchers and helped establish the institution's high-performance computing center.

Lucas and Nate add exciting new strengths to a department with growing interests in artificial intelligence, data, and the societal implications of computing. Just as importantly, both come to Williams with substantial commitments to undergraduate teaching and research.

We are very happy to welcome Lucas and Nate to Williams and look forward to having them as part of the Computer Science community.
